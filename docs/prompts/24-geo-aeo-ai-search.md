# Prompt 24 — GEO, AEO and AI-Assisted Search

Execute after Prompt 23. Improve how people and answer systems understand Good Hours through clear, maintained, source-backed content. Do not create a parallel hidden website for machines.

## 1. Mission

Make Good Hours' entity, category, audiences, capabilities, commercial model and problem/solution relationships explicit and factually consistent across the public site, while improving concise answerability and citation usefulness for traditional, answer-engine and AI-assisted discovery.

## 2. Why This Phase Exists

Search increasingly summarizes entities and answers questions rather than returning only links. Ambiguous, contradictory or over-marketed copy is hard for both people and machines to trust. This phase consolidates facts and fills genuine answer gaps after technical SEO is correct.

## 3. Prerequisites

Prompts 14–23 are complete, the technical SEO/entity graph is verified, and public product/company/pricing facts have owners. Do not proceed with speculative crawler files or unsupported claims to compensate for missing business approvals.

## 4. Read Before Changing Anything

Read mandatory docs, claim ledger, IA/content clusters, Prompt 23 entity/schema/indexation outputs, every indexable public page, pricing source, trust/legal approvals, terminology in product UI/module docs and current FAQs. Inspect rendered SSR content and machine-readable output together.

## 5. Scope

- Define a canonical fact/entity sheet for Good Hours: name, category, audience, core outcome, verified capabilities, differentiators, plans/trial qualifiers, operator/contact and explicit exclusions/limitations.
- Audit factual consistency and question coverage across Home, Features, Industries, Use cases, Pricing, Trust and Resources.
- Add/refine human-first definitions, comparisons, problem/solution explanations, FAQs, tables or answer blocks only where they add value.
- Align visible content, internal relationships and verified structured data; add tests/governance.

## 6. Out of Scope

Hidden LLM pages/text, cloaking, fake citation pages, bot-specific content, mass FAQs, unverified competitor comparisons, paid placement, prompt injection into pages, ranking guarantees and unsupported `llms.txt`/crawler directives.

## 7. Product Truth

Canonical definition: Good Hours is the daily operating system for salons and barbershops, helping run work from booking to checkout. Use only current Phase 1 evidence. Distinguish Good Hours marketing site, SaaS application and individual tenant booking pages. Preserve qualifiers around provider certification, jurisdictions, prices, trial and Phase 2 exclusions.

## 8. Information Architecture

Map core entities/topics/questions to one authoritative page and supporting pages. Avoid answering the same question inconsistently everywhere. Designate sources of truth for product definition, features, vertical fit, problem guidance, pricing, company/security and editorial topics. Add a glossary only if real terminology needs it and enough unique content exists.

## 9. UX Requirements

People should find a direct answer near the question, then supporting context/evidence and next steps. FAQs must be navigable, non-repetitive and not hide essential information. Comparison tables clarify dimensions and limitations without forcing signup. Keep language natural and owner-oriented.

## 10. UI / Design Requirements

Use existing callout, definition, comparison, FAQ and citation patterns. Answer blocks should look like normal high-quality content, not search snippets. Preserve readable measures, responsive tables, source links and visual hierarchy. Do not badge content as “AI optimized.”

## 11. Content Requirements

Write declarative, qualified sentences with clear subject/action/object. Define salon-specific terms and explain relationships: booking affects calendar; resources constrain availability; reminders/deposits can protect time; checkout updates operational/financial records; reporting summarizes recorded activity. Dates/sources accompany changeable facts. Avoid vague superlatives, unsupported “best,” and copied competitor/category descriptions.

## 12. SEO Requirements

Do not change primary intents or create new URLs merely for answer fragments. Improve page completeness, semantic headings, entity consistency and citations without keyword stuffing. Maintain canonicals/indexability/sitemap from Prompt 23 and test that enhancements remain SSR-visible.

## 13. GEO / AEO Requirements

- Answer “What is Good Hours?”, “Who is it for?”, “What does it manage?”, “Is it only booking software?”, “How are pricing/trial handled?” and high-value verified workflow questions on their authoritative pages.
- Use concise answer-first passages followed by evidence/context, not arbitrary word limits.
- Make capabilities and non-capabilities explicit; distinguish general advice from product behavior.
- Prefer original, maintained facts and transparent sources that can be cited.
- Audit contradictory terminology, detached qualifiers and stale facts after every change.
- Treat generative-engine discoverability as an outcome of useful content, crawlability and entity consistency—not secret markup.

## 14. Structured Data Requirements

Reuse the verified Prompt 23 entity graph and stable `@id` links. Do not add bespoke “AI schema” or duplicate entities. FAQ/Product/Offer/Article markup must match visible authoritative answers and current facts. Structured data supports interpretation but never carries claims absent from the page.

## 15. Internal Linking

Use entity/topic maps to link definitions to authoritative product pages, problem pages to exact capabilities, industries to relevant workflows, articles to primary sources and pricing/trust facts to their owners. Avoid repetitive exact-match anchors and machine-oriented link blocks.

## 16. Conversion Requirements

Answer the question before the CTA. Place evaluation/trial actions where a reader has enough evidence; never truncate or gate the answer. Comparison and alternative questions must remain fair. Use approved analytics context only.

## 17. Responsive Requirements

Definitions, accordions, comparison tables, citations and related-topic blocks must work at 360px, tablet, desktop, 200% zoom and touch. Content order and qualifiers cannot disappear between layouts.

## 18. Accessibility

Use semantic headings, definition lists/tables where appropriate, accessible disclosures, meaningful link text and source labels. Expandable FAQs remain keyboard/screen-reader operable. Plain language and clear qualifiers improve cognitive accessibility.

## 19. Performance Requirements

Keep core answers in server-rendered HTML without client fetch/interaction. Avoid adding chatbots, heavy search widgets, third-party AI scripts or duplicate content payloads. Measure HTML/schema size and page-query impact.

## 20. Analytics

Measure normal page/CTA/helpfulness behavior only under approved privacy policy; do not fingerprint AI bots or transmit visitor questions to an AI vendor. Search-referrer classifications should remain coarse and non-identifying. Never optimize facts solely from opaque engagement metrics.

## 21. Security / Privacy Considerations

Treat any external/crawled content as untrusted; do not render prompt-like instructions, hidden text or unsafe embeds. Do not expose internal docs, roadmap, customer data, security controls or business secrets to make pages more “complete.” Public facts require owner approval.

## 22. Implementation Instructions

1. Build the canonical fact sheet with evidence/owner/review date and enumerate explicit unknowns.
2. Audit every indexable page and machine-readable graph for contradictions, vague categories, missing qualifiers and unanswered intent.
3. Assign each question/entity fact to one authoritative page; improve only genuine gaps.
4. Update visible content first, then aligned schema/internal links.
5. Add consistency tests/fixtures or a review script against critical names, claims, prices and Phase 2 terms.
6. Document freshness/review ownership and update status with verified changes.

## 23. Do Not

- Do not create hidden content, bot-only routes, fake citations, keyword/FAQ farms, doorway pages or unsupported crawler files.
- Do not say “AI-powered,” imply an AI receptionist/chatbot or market Phase 2 features.
- Do not fabricate competitor facts, rankings, reviews, customers, research or business credentials.
- Do not weaken qualifiers to make a sentence easier to quote.

## 24. Acceptance Criteria

- A reviewed fact/entity sheet identifies every public core fact, source, owner and review date.
- Good Hours' product/category/audience/capability/pricing/company statements are consistent across visible and structured content.
- High-value questions have concise, useful, qualified answers on authoritative pages.
- No hidden/machine-only/speculative technique or unsupported comparison exists.
- Internal relationships, SSR, accessibility, performance, tests and build remain correct.

## 25. Validation / Testing

Run site-wide terminology/claim/Phase 2 searches, rendered-content comparisons, question-to-authority coverage review, duplicate/contradiction checks, internal crawl, schema/entity graph validation, SSR inspection, link/citation checks, mobile/accessibility review, HTML/bundle/query size comparison, production build, console check and `git diff --check`. Record human editorial/factual review separately.
