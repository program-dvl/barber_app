# ClipperDesk — Hamilton sales partner briefing

Prepared: 2026-11-07. Audience: the founder’s sales partner and anyone preparing client presentations or proposals. This is an internal sales reference; adapt the client-facing language below for each business.

## 1. What we are selling

**ClipperDesk helps barbershops and salons manage bookings, walk-ins, staff schedules, client information and recorded checkout in one place.**

Brand line: **Your whole day. Beautifully run.**

We are selling a simpler working day: fewer places to check, clearer appointments, easier front-desk coordination and a useful record of each visit. The software is a web-based subscription service. Customers use it through a browser; do not promise a native phone app.

Our initial approach is personal: meet local owners, understand their shop, show their actual workflow, offer a three-month pilot trial and help them set up their business in ClipperDesk. The goal is to earn continued paid use through practical value, rather than sell a long list of features.

Hamilton is the initial sales area named by the founder. Confirm the country/province before putting regional claims, local currency, tax language or contract terms into a proposal. This brief does not assume which Hamilton or claim market research about it.

## 2. Who to approach first

Prioritize independent barbershops and salons, solo professionals and single-location teams, typically with 2–15 staff. Strong early prospects combine appointments and walk-ins, coordinate bookings through several channels or rely on paper, messages and a separate payment terminal.

Speak first with the owner or person who controls scheduling and software spending. Receptionists and working barbers/stylists are important users: include them in the demonstration and setup discussion.

| Prospect situation | Useful conversation starter |
| --- | --- |
| Paper diary or scattered messages | “How do you keep phone bookings and walk-ins aligned?” |
| Busy shared reception | “What takes the most effort when several clients arrive together?” |
| Existing booking software | “What would need to improve to make switching worthwhile?” |
| New shop | “Would help setting up your services, hours and booking page be useful?” |
| Owner works alone | “How do you take bookings while you are with a client?” |

Businesses needing medical records, complex payroll, memberships or a marketplace to supply new clients are poor fits for promises we can make today. Other appointment-led industries appear in the broader product positioning, but the initial pitch should stay focused on salons and barbershops.

## 3. The business problems and product value

| Owner’s problem | What to show | Outcome to discuss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookings arrive in different places | Calendar and staff-created appointments | A shared view of the working day |
| Clients need an easier way to book | Branded public booking page | A link clients can use without calling the shop |
| Walk-ins are hard to coordinate | Walk-in queue and staff assignment | Clearer arrival and service order |
| Staff hours and services vary | Team availability and service assignments | Booking choices tied to who can deliver the service |
| Client details are difficult to find | Client profile and visit history | Better context for the next visit |
| Staff repeatedly send appointment messages | Enabled appointment confirmations and reminders | Less repeated communication work, subject to delivery and permission checks |
| Payment records are scattered | Checkout, recorded payment methods and receipts | A linked record of the visit and money received |
| Owner lacks a clear daily picture | Dashboard and operational reports | Easier review of activity and recorded sales |

These are intended benefits, not measured customer results. Do not guarantee more revenue, fewer no-shows or a number of hours saved. Establish the shop’s baseline and measure its own trial.

## 4. Product coverage and honest demonstration boundaries

The repository records implemented workflows and dated local verification. That does not certify every feature on the deployed customer account. Before each pitch, check the actual demonstration account and enabled workflows. Use fictional client data.

| Area | Sales explanation | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business setup | Configure business details, opening hours, services, staff and optional branding/photos | Owner reviews details and explicitly approves online publication |
| Scheduling | View appointments, availability and controlled appointment changes | Demonstrate supported combinations; avoid “every scheduling rule is supported” |
| Walk-ins and waitlist | Manage arriving clients and offer eligible openings | No promised VIP/temporary queue-hold workflow |
| Online booking | Share a branded booking link with client appointment-management options | Not a marketplace, traffic guarantee or full custom website service |
| Client records | Keep contact details, visit history, notes and permissions together | Collect only appropriate information; no medical compliance claims |
| Communications | Appointment emails and texts when enabled | Consent, delivery setup and text credits apply; not unlimited messaging or marketing campaigns |
| Checkout | Record money received, including supported split/partial payments, and issue receipts | Does not itself charge a client’s card or operate their card terminal |
| Refund records | Preserve recorded financial adjustments and history | Does not execute a refund through the client-payment provider |
| Inventory | Lightweight retail stock tracking | Not supplier procurement or a full retail e-commerce platform |
| Tips and commissions | Basic records and calculations | Not payroll processing, tax filing or employment advice |
| Reporting | Operational and recorded-sales visibility | Not a replacement for accounting software; do not advertise stock report screens |
| Roles and support | Permission-based access and controlled support workflows | No absolute security, legal certification or uptime guarantee |
| Reviews and visit emails | Some newer workflows exist in source | Review production gates first; do not include public reviews or new PDF/review email automation as an unconditional pilot deliverable |

**Do not sell online deposit collection as available.** The public self-service payment journey is incomplete. A deposit-required booking cannot bypass payment confirmation; use only a reviewed supported booking policy. Keep the shop’s existing terminal/payment arrangements where suitable.

Deferred features include memberships, packages, gift cards, loyalty/referrals, bulk campaigns, native apps, marketplace discovery, e-commerce, advanced payroll, franchise controls, AI receptionist and forecasting. A request is feedback, not a delivery commitment.

## 5. The Hamilton three-month pilot offer

**Founder-authorized offer:** selected initial Hamilton customers receive a three-month software trial and assistance setting up their shop.

Suggested client wording:

> We’re inviting a small group of Hamilton barbershops and salons to try ClipperDesk for three months. We’ll help you set up your shop, services, team and booking page, then work with you to see whether it fits your day-to-day business. Before you start, we’ll confirm your trial dates, what assistance is included and the price if you choose to continue.

The standard public signup currently describes a **14-day trial**, starting after email verification, with no card and no automatic charge. The three-month pilot is a separate offer: it is **not automatically activated by this document or a normal signup**. The platform team must apply it through an authorized process and verify the account’s actual expiry before the partner confirms it to the customer.

Use these safeguards when recording each offer:

- Write down the business, owner, eligible location/staff count, exact start date and expiry in the shop’s time zone. Agree whether “three months” means calendar months; do not silently substitute 90 days.
- Identify which software access is included, text allowance/usage conditions and any separate costs. Do not describe texts, payment processing or migration as unlimited or free.
- Agree the setup tasks, who performs them and any costs before work. The founder has authorized help; no fixed turnaround or unlimited ongoing service is established.
- Explain the continuation price before the trial starts. Give the owner a clear choice about paid continuation; do not imply that signing up authorizes a paid subscription.
- Confirm end-of-trial access and available export/closure options with the platform team. Do not promise indefinite access, instant deletion or a retention period.
- Record the accepted offer and confirm the configured account matches it. Do not collect card details or share passwords through the sales partner.

Before enrollment, the founder/platform team must confirm regional readiness, support ownership and the applicable customer terms. A free pilot does not establish approval for live client payment processing or live text sending.

## 6. Pricing and commercial explanation

ClipperDesk uses **one core software plan**, priced by locations and bookable staff, rather than Starter/Pro feature tiers. A bookable staff member is someone clients can book a service with. Login-only reception/admin users do not count as bookable staff.

Approved software rates in the current specification:

| Component | Monthly USD |
| --- | ---: |
| Base: one location and one bookable person | $29 |
| Each additional location | $19 |
| Bookable people in positions 2–5 | $9 each |
| Bookable people in positions 6–10 | $6 each |
| Bookable people in positions 11 onward | $4 each |

Staff bands apply across the business; the same person is counted once across branches. Annual pricing is ten times the corresponding monthly amount, charged upfront for a year. Taxes may apply. Verify the current published catalog and account quote before sending an offer; these examples are not a binding quote or live billing certification.

| Example, one location | Monthly USD before tax | Annual USD upfront before tax |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 bookable person | $29 | $290 |
| 3 bookable people | $47 | $470 |
| 5 bookable people | $65 | $650 |
| 8 bookable people | $83 | $830 |
| 15 bookable people | $115 | $1,150 |

Software billing is currently approved in **USD**. Do not label these amounts CAD, NZD or another local currency. The shop’s sales currency is a separate setting. Card issuer conversion charges may apply where relevant; do not quote an exchange rate.

The current subscription specification describes 100 monthly text credits per bookable staff member. Credits are consumed by message segments, so a long text can use more than one credit. Verify the pilot’s actual allowance and live delivery eligibility. Paid extra text packs are not currently enabled in the approved catalog; do not sell them.

Business model: recurring software subscription, with personal setup assistance helping customers reach useful daily operation. No partner commission, revenue share, exclusivity, sales territory rights or equity arrangement is established here. The founder and partner should agree those separately before incurring expenses or signing commitments.

## 7. Sales conversation and discovery

Start with the owner’s workflow, then show only what matters to it.

1. How do clients book today: phone, messages, online or walk-in?
2. How many locations and people actually take bookings?
3. What causes the most disruption on a busy day?
4. How do you manage staff hours, changes and arrivals?
5. What software and payment terminal do you already use?
6. What would make a three-month trial worthwhile?
7. Which records would need to move, and who can authorize that?
8. Who decides, who needs training and when would a safe start be?

Record the main problem, current tools, essential requirements, objections and agreed next action. Do not ask for client lists during the first sales conversation.

### A short pitch

> ClipperDesk brings bookings, walk-ins, staff schedules and client history into one workspace for your shop. You can give clients a booking link, organize the working day and keep a record of checkout without relying on several disconnected places. We’re offering selected Hamilton shops a three-month trial with setup help, so you can judge it using your own workflow before deciding whether to continue.

### A practical 15-minute demo

| Time | Show | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 minutes | Confirm the owner’s main problem | “Which part of a busy day should we focus on?” |
| 3–6 minutes | Services, team and booking page on a phone | “Does this match how clients choose and book?” |
| 6–9 minutes | Calendar, arrival and walk-in flow | “Would your team know what to do next?” |
| 9–12 minutes | Client history, recorded checkout and reports | “What would you need to see at the end of the day?” |
| 12–15 minutes | Setup plan, pilot dates and continuation estimate | “What needs checking before we schedule setup?” |

Use a tested fictional shop. Explain that sample appointments and amounts are illustrations. Keep payment collection separate from recording an externally received payment. If a workflow is unavailable, record it for follow-up instead of improvising a promise.

## 8. Handling common objections

| Owner says | Suggested response |
| --- | --- |
| “We already use another system.” | “What works well, and what still takes effort? Let’s compare your essential workflow before discussing a move.” |
| “Switching is too much work.” | “We can review your setup and the records you need. We’ll agree what can move and when before changing anything.” |
| “My team isn’t technical.” | “Let’s show the actual booking, arrival and checkout steps, then include your team in setup and training.” |
| “Will you bring me customers?” | “You receive a booking link for your existing channels. Marketplace discovery and guaranteed leads are not part of the offer.” |
| “Can it take card payments or deposits?” | “Checkout records payments received elsewhere. We aren’t offering online deposit collection in this pilot; we’ll check your existing payment workflow.” |
| “Is everything free for three months?” | “The offer covers the agreed software trial and setup assistance. We’ll write down any messaging, migration or other separate costs before you start.” |
| “What happens afterwards?” | “You can review the results and choose whether to subscribe. We’ll confirm the price and account options before your trial ends.” |
| “Can you add this feature?” | “I’ll record the requirement and get a clear answer. It isn’t included unless we explicitly agree it.” |
| “Is it compliant and secure?” | “The product includes access controls and separate business workspaces. We can share the current policies and review your requirements; I won’t claim a certification we don’t hold.” |

## 9. Assisted setup and rollout

The partner owns discovery, coordination and relationship follow-up. The founder/platform team owns account eligibility, trial configuration, technical issues and confirmation of unsupported requirements. The customer owns business accuracy, lawful use of client information and final publication approval.

1. **Agree fit and offer.** Confirm essential workflows, trial dates, continuation estimate and support contact.
2. **Create the owner’s account.** The owner verifies their own email and retains control of credentials. The platform team verifies the special trial.
3. **Prepare the shop.** Review name, contact details, address, local time zone, operating currency, opening hours, services, prices, durations, team, service assignments and permissions.
4. **Review existing records.** Clients and upcoming appointments can be assessed for assisted migration. Other records require individual review. Agree source format, scope, timing, costs, authorized transfer and validation first. Do not promise an automated importer or universal compatibility.
5. **Rehearse.** Test a booking, staff-created appointment, walk-in and recorded checkout. Verify actual email/text behavior where enabled, including phone usability and appointment change notices.
6. **Approve publication.** Owner reviews booking page, policies, services and availability on desktop and phone, then explicitly approves going live.
7. **Start with controlled use.** Agree the cutover and which system owns upcoming bookings. Avoid two systems silently accepting the same appointment slot. Retain the old records safely until the agreed move is checked.
8. **Train and follow up.** Practice daily actions with the people who will use them. Record issues and escalate blockers promptly.

Never import client information into a shared demo, request passwords, transfer client lists through personal messaging or promise deletion of financial history. Get authorized help through the agreed secure process.

Suggested rhythm: setup meeting, first-week check-in, end-of-month review and a continuation discussion two to three weeks before expiry. Adjust to the shop; this is a working plan, not a contracted support SLA.

## 10. Proving value during the trial

Agree two or three measures with each owner, using their starting point:

- Are staff using one agreed calendar consistently?
- Can clients complete the supported booking journey on a phone?
- How much time does the shop spend organizing appointments each week?
- Are walk-ins and arrivals easier for the team to coordinate?
- Can the owner find visit and recorded-payment information without searching several places?
- What prevented normal use, and was it resolved?
- Does the owner consider the confirmed paid price worthwhile?

Compare like periods and record the method. Trial feedback is not permission to publish a testimonial. Get explicit approval for any quote, shop name, image or case study.

Recommended pitch deck:

1. ClipperDesk and the owner’s main problem.
2. A familiar busy-day example, clearly labeled as illustrative.
3. The linked journey: booking → arrival → service → recorded checkout → client history.
4. Real product screenshots with fictional data.

7. Three-month Hamilton pilot, dates and included scope.

9. What the trial will measure.

A client proposal should include the business and decision-maker, stated needs, agreed deliverables and exclusions, exact dates, location/staff quantities, USD pricing/taxes, separate costs, migration scope, customer responsibilities, support contact, paid-continuation choice and applicable approved terms. Do not convert this internal brief into a legal contract without review.

Use product evidence rather than invented customer counts, revenue figures, testimonials, competitor comparisons or market-size claims. No researched Hamilton market statistics or competitor pricing are supplied here.

## 12. Partner working checklist

- Maintain a simple prospect list: business, authorized contact, needs, stage, next action and follow-up date. Respect contact preferences.
- Keep stages practical: identified → discovery → demonstrated → offer agreed → setup → active pilot → paid/declined.
- Coordinate follow-up so the founder and partner do not send conflicting offers.
- Escalate payment, privacy, data-loss, cross-business visibility and booking failures immediately through the agreed support path.
- Confirm the configured support mailbox is monitored before sharing it. Repository default: support@clipperdesk.com. Do not promise response times or round-the-clock support without agreement.
- Review pilot progress with the founder weekly. Track blockers as well as signups; an account created is not proof the shop is successfully using it.
